I think you're aiming for something much stronger than "build an AI model." You're aiming to build a product that ordinary people can trust. That changes many of the engineering decisions.

I would rewrite the project's vision like this:

AdharaAI should enable any Indian citizen—including a 16-year-old student with basic smartphone skills—to understand a legal document confidently, without needing prior legal knowledge.

That is a measurable product goal.

Goal 1: Extreme User-Centric Design

Instead of asking, "Can the AI analyze the document?", ask:

Can a first-time user understand what to do within 30 seconds?

Some design principles I'd recommend:

1. Never use legal language without explaining it

Instead of:

❌ Lock-in Period

Use:

✅ You cannot leave this agreement for 11 months without paying a penalty.

Instead of:

❌ Indemnity Clause

Use:

✅ If something goes wrong, you may have to pay for the other person's losses.

2. Every risk should answer three questions

Every clause card should have:

What does this mean?

(Simple explanation)

Why should I care?

(Real-world consequence)

What should I do?

(Actionable advice)

Example:

⚠ High Risk

Meaning

"You cannot terminate this agreement for one year."

Why it matters

"If you leave early, you may have to pay several months of rent."

What you should do

"Ask the landlord if the lock-in period can be reduced."

Now the user never has to guess.

3. Reduce reading

Use

icons
colors
progress bars
checklists

instead of long paragraphs.

4. Every screen should answer only one question

Upload page

What document do you want to understand?

Analysis page

Is this document safe?

Results page

What should I do next?

5. Make it impossible to misuse

Don't assume users know

PDF
OCR
Clause
Metadata
AI

Instead say

Upload your document

We read it

We explain it

Goal 2: Trustworthy AI instead of "100% Accuracy"

You mentioned something very important.

Claude is correct.

No real-world ML system reaches 100% accuracy on unseen data.

Even GPT-5, Gemini, Claude, and legal AI systems don't.

Instead, your goal should be:

The application should never confidently give wrong advice.

This is more important than chasing 100%.

A trustworthy AI knows when it is uncertain.

For example:

High confidence

"I'm confident this is a termination clause."

Medium confidence

"This appears to be a termination clause, but the wording is unusual."

Low confidence

"I'm not confident about this clause. Consider consulting a legal professional."

That is much safer.

Goal 3: Prevent Overfitting

This should become one of your engineering principles.

Instead of

maximize training accuracy

aim for

maximize generalization.

Ways to achieve this include:

Diverse legal documents from different Indian states.
Train/validation/test split with no document overlap.
Cross-validation where appropriate.
Early stopping during fine-tuning.
Regularization techniques.
Evaluate on documents never seen during training.
Test on deliberately challenging cases (unusual wording, OCR errors, mixed languages).
Goal 4: Robustness

Ask:

"What can break my system?"

Then deliberately test it.

Examples:

Low-quality scans
Rotated PDFs
Watermarks
Handwritten notes
Spelling mistakes
Different fonts
Hindi
Kannada
English
Mixed-language documents
Very long agreements
One-page agreements
Missing signatures
Blank pages
Corrupted PDFs

If the application handles these gracefully, users will trust it more.

Goal 5: Human-Centered Evaluation

Don't only measure ML metrics.

Also measure user outcomes.

For example:

Metric	Target
User understands the document	>90%
User can identify the biggest risk	>90%
User completes analysis without help	>95%
Average upload time	<30 seconds
Average analysis time	<10 seconds
First-time users succeed	>95%
User satisfaction	>4.7/5

These product metrics matter just as much as precision and recall.

Goal 6: Build a Product People Trust

Your application should behave more like a careful legal assistant than an all-knowing expert.

That means:

Explain every conclusion.
Highlight the exact text that triggered a risk.
Show confidence where appropriate.
Encourage review when confidence is low.
Never invent legal facts or case law.
Clearly state that the app provides informational assistance, not legal representation.
My Recommendation

If I were leading AdharaAI, I'd define the mission this way:

Mission: Empower every Indian citizen to understand legal documents in plain language, regardless of age or legal knowledge.

And I'd establish these non-negotiable engineering principles:

User First: A first-time user should understand and use the app without training.
Explainability: Every output must include the reasoning and relevant document text.
Honesty: The AI should express uncertainty instead of making unsupported claims.
Robustness: The system should handle messy, real-world documents gracefully.
Generalization: Optimize for performance on unseen documents, not just the training set.
Safety: Minimize the chance of harmful or misleading advice by combining AI with rule-based checks where appropriate.
Accessibility: Support clear language, mobile devices, and major Indian languages so the app serves as many people as possible.

If your team consistently designs and evaluates against these principles, AdharaAI will stand out not because it claims "100% accuracy," but because it is reliable, understandable, and genuinely useful to the people it is built for.
